Why %HRR and %VO₂R rather than %VO₂max: at rest a person is at 0% HRR but somewhere between 7% and 14% of VO₂max depending on fitness. Prescribing off %VO₂max therefore gives two clients of different fitness levels genuinely different training stimuli. ACSM switched to %VO₂R in 1998 for exactly this reason — and a lot of published research still gets it wrong.
The formulas you actually need

Max heart rate — Tanaka

HRmax = 208 − (0.7 × age)

More accurate than 220−age, especially over 40. A 45-year-old: 208 − 31.5 = 177 bpm, not 175.

Max heart rate — Gellish

HRmax = 207 − (0.7 × age)

Very close to Tanaka. Either is fine. Both beat 220−age, which underestimates older clients by 10+ bpm.

Karvonen — target HR from %HRR

Target = ((HRmax − HRrest) × %) + HRrest

The one to use. Requires a true resting HR — taken lying down, before getting out of bed, over 3 mornings.

MET → calories per hour

kcal/hr = METs × 3.5 × kg ÷ 200 × 60

A 70 kg client at 6 METs burns roughly 441 kcal/hr. Useful for setting a realistic cardio expectation.

VO₂R target

Target VO₂ = ((VO₂max − 3.5) × %) + 3.5

3.5 ml/kg/min is 1 MET — resting oxygen uptake. This is what makes VO₂R different from VO₂max.

Estimating VO₂max from HR

VO₂max ≈ 15 × (HRmax ÷ HRrest)

Uth-Sørensen field estimate. Rough, but free, and good enough to track change over months.

Fast mental math — the anchors worth memorising

The four numbers

P = 4 kcal/g · C = 4 kcal/g
F = 9 kcal/g · Alcohol = 7 kcal/g

Fat is more than twice as dense as carbs. This is why the oil in a sabzi matters more than the sabzi.

Roti anchor

1 roti ≈ 100 kcal, 3.5 g protein

Four rotis = 400 kcal and only 14 g protein. Most Indian meals are carb-heavy and protein-poor.

Rice anchor

1 katori ≈ 200 kcal, 4 g protein

One katori of rice ≈ two rotis. Clients almost never believe this until you show them.

Dal anchor

1 katori ≈ 145 kcal, 8 g protein

Dal is the protein in most Indian vegetarian diets — and one katori only gives 8 g. Do that math out loud.

Oil anchor

1 tsp = 45 kcal · 1 tbsp = 135 kcal

The single biggest hidden variable in home cooking. Always ask how much oil goes in the pan.

Milk anchor

1 glass toned ≈ 118 kcal, 6.4 g protein

Two glasses a day is 13 g protein — a genuine contribution for a vegetarian client.

Worked example. Client says: "3 roti, 1 katori dal, 1 katori sabzi, 1 katori curd."
→ Roti 300 kcal / 10.8 P  ·  Dal 145 / 8 P  ·  Sabzi 90 / 2.5 P  ·  Curd 90 / 5.3 P
→ Total ≈ 625 kcal, 27 g protein. Reasonable calories for one meal, but protein is thin. Your intervention: swap the curd for 150 g Greek yogurt or add 50 g paneer, and you add 10–12 g protein for almost no extra calories.

The 0.5–1% rule (Helms 2014): losing more than ~1% of body weight per week reliably costs lean mass. In athletes on restriction, a 0.7%/week group lost 31% of their weight as fat mass; the 1.4%/week group lost only 21% as fat. Faster is not better — it is just faster, and more of what is lost is muscle.

Body fat percentage — time to drop it
Careful here — the arithmetic is not what people assume. Dropping body fat by 5 percentage points requires losing more than 5% of body weight in fat, because lean mass stays constant while the total shrinks. A 70 kg client at 25% BF must lose 4.4 kg of fat to reach 20%, not 3.5 kg.

Measurement honesty. Every body fat method has meaningful error: DEXA ±3%, BIA scales ±5–8% (and swing wildly with hydration), calipers ±3–5% in trained hands and worse in untrained ones. Use the same method, same conditions, same time of day and track the direction of change. Never quote an absolute body fat number as if it were precise.
