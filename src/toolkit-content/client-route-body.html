C.L.I.E.N.T.
The 6-Step System Personal Trainers Use to Turn First Sessions Into Long-Term Clients

The order I actually think in when a client walks through the door, before a single set gets programmed.

Most new trainers meet a client, hear "I want to get fit," and jump straight to writing a program. I did the same thing early on. But the program should be the last move, not the first. Before I design a single set, I run through six steps — C.L.I.E.N.T. Skip any one of them, and you don't lose reps, you lose the client.

1

Check

Before you say a word about training

Ask about current activity level and any known cardiovascular, metabolic, or renal issues, or symptoms (chest pain, dizziness, unusual shortness of breath). This comes before goals — every time.

Why it's step oneAnything that flags here changes everything downstream. Skipping it because it feels awkward is how new trainers get themselves and their clients hurt.
2

Look

Watch before you load

Watch them squat, hinge, reach overhead. Does the low back round? Do shoulders shrug instead of the shoulder blades gliding? This tells you whether they start with foundational movement work or go straight to load.

Why it's step twoSkip this and you load a compensation pattern straight into someone — and bake in an injury before you've even started programming.
3

Inquire

The goal behind the goal

"Get fit" and "tone up" are greetings, not goals. Ask one more question: what does that look like, is there a date attached, what's it actually for. Translate the real answer into training-science terms yourself.

Why it's step threeProgram off the surface answer and you'll build something technically correct that misses what the client actually cares about — and you lose them even while they're getting stronger.
4

Ease In

Lighter than you think you need

Low volume, low-to-moderate intensity, full-body if beginner. Stay two gears below what they can probably handle in week one — no exceptions, no matter how ready they look.

Why it's step fourDOMS from an overambitious week one is the single biggest reason people quit in week two. You're buying trust, not maximizing stimulus.
5

Navigate

Now the variables come in

Frequency, volume, intensity, tempo, rest, exercise selection — manipulated deliberately against the specific goal. This is what most new trainers think training starts with. It's actually step five.

Why it's step fiveEverything upstream (screen, see, say, set) is what makes this step safe and effective instead of a generic template.
6

Track

Reassess on a rhythm

Every few weeks: same goal? New pain? Enjoying it or gritting through it? Progress with double progression as readiness shows. If they miss time, meet them where they currently are — don't punish with a jump back to where they left off.

Why it's step sixThis is reversibility in practice — the plan has to bend with real life or adherence breaks.
